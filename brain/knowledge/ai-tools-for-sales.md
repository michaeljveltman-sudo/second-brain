## Research Workflow

### For Prospect Research (Inbound Lead Came In)
**Tool**: Perplexity + LinkedIn + Claude

**Prompt: Quick Company Brief**
```
Research [Company Name] and give me:
1. What they do in 2 sentences
2. Their likely biggest challenge right now
3. What kind of Toptal talent they might need (dev / design / finance / PM)
4. One recent news item that's relevant
5. Likely buyer profile at this company (role + their priorities)
Keep it to 5 bullets. I have a call in 20 minutes.
```

**Prompt: Contact Profile**
```
What can you find on [Name], [Title] at [Company]?
I want: career history highlights, what they're focused on right now (based on recent posts/news), and what they probably care about most in their role.
Keep it brief — I need this for a discovery call prep.
```

## The Call Audit Framework (Taught to Teams + Students)
Michael uses this with his Toptal team and teaches it in AI for Sales:

**"The Unasked Questions" Audit:**
1. Upload call transcript to Zoom AI or Gong AI
2. Ask: "List every moment where a deeper question would have improved pain point insight, trust, or clarity."
3. Categorise: Discovery / Budget / Authority / Timeline / Emotional drivers
4. Rewrite responses using the top 3 missing questions
5. Apply on the next call

**Why it works**: AI doesn't make you better at sales. It makes your gaps visible. The rep has to act on it.

## Writing & Communication

### For Follow-Up Emails
**Tool**: Claude (with voice context)
```
Write a follow-up email after my call with [Name] at [Company].
What we discussed: [brief notes]
My commitment: [what I said I'd do]
Their next step: [what they agreed to]
Rules: Max 5 sentences. One ask. Sign off as "Michael". No fluff.
My voice: Direct, warm, specific. Not corporate.
```

### For LinkedIn Posts
**Tool**: Claude (with style guide context)
```
Turn this [story/insight/observation] into a LinkedIn post.
My voice: Takes a clear position. Short lines. White space. Real example required.
Hook must create genuine tension or curiosity — earns the "see more."
Max 250 words.
Two options: one safer, one bolder.
```

## Pipeline Analysis

### Weekly Pipeline Review Prompt
```
Review my pipeline and tell me:
1. Which deals have had no activity in 7+ days?
2. Which deals have no "next step with a date"?
3. Which deals are past their expected close date?
4. Top 3 deals I should focus on this week — and why
5. Any deals I should consider disqualifying?

Pipeline data: [paste or pull from HubSpot]
Context: Toptal EMEA SMB inbound. Qualification = MEDDPICC adapted for inbound.
```

### Coaching Observation Prompt
```
Here are call notes/transcripts from my team this week.
Identify:
1. Common patterns — what questions are reps NOT asking?
2. Which reps are moving to proposal before qualifying urgency?
3. What's the most common point where deals are stalling?
Tone: Coaching observation, not judgement. I'll use this for my 1:1s.
```

## Data Quality (Research Insight)
From Michael's EU startup research: A startup's AI forecast accuracy jumped 22% simply by cleaning their CRM data — fixing duplicates, standardising naming conventions, ensuring company emails had their own column.

**The lesson**: AI doesn't fix messy data. It magnifies it.
**Pre-AI data checklist** (teach this to students and teams):
- [ ] Clean naming conventions (ACME Ltd. vs Acme Limited — pick one)
- [ ] Complete outcomes in every deal (AI can't learn from blanks)
- [ ] Consistent data entry habits across the team
- [ ] Campaign results tracked back to deals (attribution matters)

## What Michael Teaches (AI for Sales Track)

### The Core Tool Stack for Students
1. Perplexity — real-time research with citations
2. Claude / GPT-4o — drafting, prompting, analysis
3. Zoom AI / Gong AI — call analysis and coaching
4. HubSpot basics — CRM discipline before AI can help
5. Zapier/make.com — automating the repetitive stuff

### The Key Prompting Framework Michael Uses (From Posts)
```
1. Give context: "You're an AI assistant helping a sales manager who works in [industry]..."
2. Set the task: "Summarise key discussion points from this coaching session..."
3. Be specific: "Highlight coaching gaps. Under 200 words."
4. Add correlation: "Suggest one idea that could improve pipeline velocity."
5. Follow-up: "What should I measure next time to deepen this analysis?"
```

His principle: "Prompting is just structured thinking — it trains the model and your own clarity."
