# Quality Standards — What "Good" Looks Like

## The Core Test
Would I be comfortable if this went to the client / student / my network right now without any edits?

If yes: send it.
If no: what specifically needs to change?

---

## Email Quality Standard

**A great email from Michael:**
- Under 5 sentences in the body
- One clear ask
- Subject line is specific (contains the client's name or a specific detail)
- No banned words
- Sign-off: just "Michael"
- Zero chance of being mistaken for a mass email

**Grading:**
- 5/5: Send immediately
- 4/5: Minor tweak needed — adjust the one thing and send
- 3/5 or below: Rewrite — something is fundamentally off

---

## Proposal Quality Standard

**A great proposal:**
- Client's specific pain is named in the first paragraph
- Executive summary tells the whole story in 4 sentences
- Solution section is specific to them, not generic
- Has quantified outcomes or proof points
- Clear single next step with a date
- No section that could have been copy-pasted from a template without changes

---

## LinkedIn Post Quality Standard

**A great post:**
- Hook earns the "see more" click (counterintuitive, specific, or bold)
- Body delivers on the promise of the hook
- Contains a real example or story (not a platitude)
- One clear takeaway the reader can act on
- Not trying too hard — sounds like Michael, not a content creator

**Red flags:**
- Starts with "I" (most feeds suppress this)
- More than 3 consecutive lines without a break
- No concrete example anywhere in the post
- Ends with a generic "what do you think?" CTA

---

## Teaching Content Quality Standard

**A great lesson:**
- Hook creates genuine curiosity in 30 seconds
- Every concept has a real sales example (no abstract theories)
- Student knows what to DO differently after the lesson
- Exercise is specific and completable in < 20 min
- The "so what" is explicit — why does this matter to their career?

---

## Discovery Call Quality Standard

**A great discovery call leaves you with:**
- Clear understanding of their #1 pain (can you articulate it back in 1 sentence?)
- Sense of urgency level (are they bleeding or just curious?)
- Knowledge of who decides
- A next step with a specific date on the calendar

**If you leave without any of these: the call wasn't complete.**
