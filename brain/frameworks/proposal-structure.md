# Proposal Structure Framework

## The Golden Rule
A proposal is not a brochure. It's a mirror — they should read it and feel like you understood them completely.

## When to Send a Proposal
Only after discovery. A proposal sent before qualification is a brochure, not a sales tool.
Minimum required before proposing:
- [ ] Pain clearly identified and quantified
- [ ] Decision maker identified
- [ ] Timeline and budget directionally confirmed
- [ ] They've agreed to receive and review a proposal by a specific date

---

## Proposal Structure

### 1. Executive Summary (4 sentences max)
- Sentence 1: Their situation (show you listened)
- Sentence 2: The core challenge (name it specifically)
- Sentence 3: What you're proposing and why it fits
- Sentence 4: The outcome they can expect

### 2. Understanding Your Challenge
- Restate what they told you — in their language, not ours
- Include specific details from discovery (numbers, timelines, names)
- 3-5 bullet points. Not a wall of text.
- End with: "If this reflects your situation, here's how we'd approach it."

### 3. Proposed Solution
**For Toptal engagements:**
- Role profile (specific, not generic)
- Our sourcing approach for this need
- Expected timeline to shortlist
- How the engagement works (trial period, ongoing management, etc.)

**For teaching engagements:**
- Program name and format
- Who it's designed for (specific, not everyone)
- What they'll be able to do after (outcomes, not topics)
- Delivery timeline and structure

### 4. Why Toptal / Why Michael
- 2-3 proof points relevant to THEIR situation
- Specific client example from similar industry or use case
- Don't list every credential — choose the one most relevant to them

### 5. Investment
- Clear pricing (ranges are fine if exact TBD)
- What's included
- What's not included
- Payment terms

### 6. Next Steps
**One clear next step with a date.**
Wrong: "Let us know if you have questions."
Right: "I'd suggest a 30-minute call on [date] to walk through this together and answer your questions. Does [time] work?"

---

## Formatting Rules
- Max 2 pages for most proposals (unless it's complex)
- Bullets over paragraphs
- Client's name/company mentioned at least 3x
- No generic sections that could apply to any client
- Font: Clean, professional. No clip art. No stock photos.

---

## Common Proposal Mistakes
1. Sending before real discovery — becomes a brochure
2. Writing about yourself instead of them
3. No quantified outcome ("better results" vs "cut time-to-hire by 6 weeks")
4. Weak next step — "let me know if you have questions" kills momentum
5. Sending to the wrong person (not the economic buyer)
