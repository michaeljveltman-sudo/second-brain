# Toptal Sales Process — EMEA SMB Inbound

## Key Context
This is INBOUND sales. Leads come to us. That changes everything:
- Don't pitch — clarify
- Don't convince — qualify
- The job in discovery is to understand urgency and fit, not create interest (they already have it)
- Speed matters more than in enterprise: SMB buyers move fast or not at all

---

## Stage Definitions

### Stage 1: New Lead (Inbound)
- Lead came in via marketing, referral, or inbound channel
- Initial qualification: is this a real opportunity?
- **Exit criteria**: First call booked, basic ICP confirmed

### Stage 2: Discovery
- Qualification call completed or in progress
- Working to understand: why now, what they need, who decides, what's the real urgency
- **Exit criteria**: Opportunity qualified (clear pain, real budget direction, decision-maker access, urgency confirmed)

### Stage 3: Evaluation
- Client is actively evaluating Toptal
- Reviewing our model, understanding how we work, possibly reviewing talent profiles
- **Exit criteria**: Verbal buy-in, moving to commercial conversation

### Stage 4: Commercial
- Proposal sent, MSA/contract in discussion
- Paper process underway
- **Exit criteria**: Signed contract / engagement started

### Stage 5: Closed Won / Closed Lost
- Won: Active engagement started
- Lost: Dead. Always document the loss reason — this is coaching data.

## Inbound-Specific Plays

### Play 1: Speed Close (Most Common in SMB)
**When**: Inbound lead with urgent need (< 3-4 weeks to start)
**Approach**: Validate urgency in discovery. If real, move fast — skip the slow evaluation. "We can have a shortlist of 3 candidates to you within 5 business days of signing." Trial engagement as the first step.
**Watch-out**: Don't skip qualification. Urgency without budget or decision-maker is a time drain.

### Play 2: Subscription/Marketplace Upsell
**When**: Client on legacy model, or new client who fits one of our newer commercial models
**Approach**: Understand their hiring cadence. If they hire regularly, subscription makes more sense than one-off. Position based on their pattern, not our preference.

### Play 3: Expansion (Existing Clients)
**When**: Client has one active engagement going well
**Approach**: Quarterly check-in. "How is [talent name] working out? What else is on your roadmap?" Expand to adjacent team or role.
**Signals to watch**: Client mentions new projects, team growth, a problem outside the current engagement.

### Play 4: Competitive Replacement (Upwork / Agency)
**When**: Prospect mentions using Upwork, Fiverr, or a staffing agency and is frustrated
**Approach**: Don't bash the competitor. Ask: "What made you look at alternatives?" Then: "The main difference is vetting — 3% of applicants pass our process. The first 3 candidates we send are usually as good as or better than the 10th candidate you'd interview elsewhere."

### Play 5: Stuck Deal Revival
**When**: Deal went quiet after discovery or proposal
**Approach**: Don't "just follow up." Re-engage with something new — a relevant client story, a market insight, a changed circumstance. "Something you said about [specific thing] stayed with me — has anything changed on that front?"

## Objection Quick Reference (Inbound Context)

| Objection | What's Really Going On | Response |
|-----------|------------------------|----------|
| "Too expensive" | Doesn't see the ROI yet | "What's a 6-week hiring delay costing you in deliverables right now?" |
| "We hire directly" | Happy with status quo | "How long is a direct hire taking at the moment? What's on hold while you wait?" |
| "Not ready yet" | No urgency or internal blocker | "What has to happen first? What's the trigger that would make this the right time?" |
| "We tried Upwork" | Wrong comparison, bad experience | "We're not a marketplace. Our vetting means you avoid the 'joined yesterday, put make.com on their skills' problem." |
| "Need to check budget" | Missing economic buyer | "Who else needs to be part of this conversation? Happy to set up a call that works for everyone." |
| "Send me info first" | Wants to delay | "Happy to. So I make it relevant — what specifically do you need to see to move forward?" |

## Coaching Notes (for Team Management)

### What good looks like in discovery
- Rep can summarise the client's #1 problem in one sentence after the call
- Next step is specific with a date — not "I'll send you info"
- Rep knows who the economic buyer is before sending a proposal

### Common team gaps to coach
- Moving to proposal before qualifying urgency (asking "when do you need someone by?" is not enough)
- Not asking who else is part of the decision
- Following up with "just checking in" instead of something value-adding
- Not logging loss reasons (this kills coaching opportunities)

### Weekly rhythm
- Monday: pipeline review — what's moving, what's stuck, what needs a next step
- Real-time coaching via Zoom AI / Gong AI call summaries
- Bi-weekly Crash Course + Catalyst Call format
- 1:1s focused on deal review and skill development, not just status updates

## CRM (HubSpot) Discipline
- Stage updated within 24 hours of every conversation
- Every deal must have a next step WITH a date — if there isn't one, it's not an active deal
- Loss reason documented for every closed lost (required, not optional)
- Call notes summarised and saved (Zoom AI / Gong AI for automatic summaries)
- Clean data = better AI analysis. Garbage in, garbage out.
