# Sales for AI People — Curriculum

## Program Overview
**Target audience**: AI/tech founders, ML engineers going to market, AI consultants, technical co-founders — people who are great at building but struggle to sell.
**Core promise**: You'll learn to sell the way the best salespeople do — by being genuinely helpful, not by being pushy.
**Prerequisite**: Some experience trying to sell (even one client conversation).
**Format**: [Fill in — cohort / 1:1 coaching / workshop]
**Duration**: [Fill in]

---

## The Core Insight to Teach
Technical people often fail at sales not because they can't communicate — but because they pitch when they should be listening. Sales is discovery, not presentation.

---

## Learning Outcomes
By the end, students will be able to:
1. Have a discovery conversation without turning it into a product demo
2. Write outbound that gets replies (not just impressions)
3. Build and maintain a simple pipeline
4. Handle common objections without getting defensive
5. Close a deal using a process, not pressure
6. Build a repeatable sales motion for their product

---

## Module Structure

### Module 1: The Mindset Shift — Selling ≠ Convincing
**Learning outcome**: Understand what sales actually is (hint: it's mostly listening).
**Key concepts**:
- The difference between selling and helping
- Why technical founders default to pitching (and why it backfires)
- Sales as problem discovery, not product discovery
- The best salespeople are curious, not charismatic
**Exercise**: Recall your last "sales" conversation. What were you doing? What should you have been doing?

---

### Module 2: Finding the Right People
**Learning outcome**: Define your ICP and find real prospects this week.
**Key concepts**:
- ICP vs. "anyone who might buy" — why specificity matters
- Where your first 10 customers actually come from (not cold email)
- Using LinkedIn, communities, and existing networks
- What makes a good lead vs. a waste of time
**Exercise**: Define your ICP in 1 paragraph. Find 20 real people who fit it.

---

### Module 3: Outreach That Doesn't Feel Like Spam
**Learning outcome**: Write cold outreach that gets a real response.
**Key concepts**:
- The only reason people reply to cold outreach (relevance + specificity)
- The 4-sentence email framework
- Subject lines that work vs. subject lines that get deleted
- Volume vs. quality (the right answer for early-stage)
**Exercise**: Write 5 real outreach emails to real prospects. Send them.

---

### Module 4: The Discovery Call — Your Most Important Skill
**Learning outcome**: Run a discovery conversation that makes the prospect feel understood.
**Key concepts**:
- How to open a discovery call (set the agenda — don't just start talking)
- The SPIN questioning framework (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff)
- Listening more than you talk — what this actually looks like
- The demo trap: why demos too early kill deals
- How to close the call with a next step, not "I'll send you info"
**Exercise**: Do a discovery call role-play with a partner. Record and review it.

---

### Module 5: Presenting Without Pitching
**Learning outcome**: Present your solution in a way that maps directly to what they told you.
**Key concepts**:
- The rule: only show what's relevant to their stated problems
- "Problem → Solution" framing (not "Feature → Feature")
- Handling the "can you give me a demo?" early in the process
- ROI framing for technical products (connecting features to business outcomes)
**Exercise**: Rewrite your standard product pitch as a "problem → solution" narrative

---

### Module 6: Objections Are Just Questions
**Learning outcome**: Handle the 5 most common objections without flinching.
**Key concepts**:
- Objections = unresolved questions, not rejections
- The "feel / felt / found" framework (and when not to use it)
- The 5 common early-stage objections and how to handle them:
  - "Too expensive"
  - "Not the right time"
  - "We can build this ourselves"
  - "Let me check with [X]"
  - "Send me more info"
**Exercise**: Roleplay each objection. Find your natural response.

---

### Module 7: Closing — Making It Easy to Say Yes
**Learning outcome**: Close deals without feeling pushy.
**Key concepts**:
- Closing is not a technique — it's a logical outcome of good discovery
- The "next step close" — every conversation ends with a next step
- When to use urgency (and when it backfires)
- How to re-engage a prospect who went quiet
- The "lose fast" principle — why disqualifying quickly is valuable
**Exercise**: Map your current pipeline. For each deal: what's the next step? If there isn't one, it's not a deal.

---

### Module 8: Building a Sales Motion That Scales
**Learning outcome**: Have a repeatable, simple sales process you can run consistently.
**Key concepts**:
- Pipeline stages: what they are and why they matter
- CRM basics (you don't need Salesforce — you need discipline)
- Win/loss analysis: learning from every deal
- When to hire your first salesperson (and how to not hire wrong)
- Using AI tools to make your solo sales process faster
**Exercise**: Build your personal pipeline in a simple spreadsheet. Put every active conversation in it.

---

## Teaching Notes
- Use technical analogies where helpful (pipeline = CI/CD for leads, etc.)
- Acknowledge imposter syndrome — these people are smart, they just feel out of their element
- Be honest about how sales actually works — strip away the mystique
- Real examples from AI startups are most powerful
- Cover rejection — this is the #1 fear for technical people
