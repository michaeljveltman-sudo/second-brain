## Core Writing Principles

### 1. Take a position. Every time.
Michael's own words: "Are you sharing information… or taking a position?"
No "here are both sides." No "it depends." Pick a lane.

Wrong: "AI is changing sales in many ways."
Right: "AI didn't just change how you sell. It changed what your buyer is afraid of before you've said a word."

### 2. Short lines. White space. Always.
Every line break is intentional. Single sentence per line when making a point.

Wrong (wall of text):
> AI is transforming the sales process by enabling reps to research prospects faster, draft communications more efficiently, and analyse pipeline with greater precision.

Right:
> AI makes you faster.
> Curiosity makes you trusted.
> Empathy closes the loop.

### 3. Open mid-scene or with something uncomfortable
Don't ease in. Drop the reader into the moment.

Real openers that worked:
- "Yesterday I watched a sales rep try to close me..."
- '"Because ChatGPT told me so."'
- "Breaking - ChatGPT just laid off the em dash..."
- "Stop explaining what you do. Start explaining what your prospect gets back."
- "The sales conversation has changed. Most people haven't noticed yet."

### 4. Self-deprecating beats self-promoting
He jokes about using Safari. He admits his LinkedIn was flat for weeks before it worked. The post about losing his brother is his most human and most trusted. Vulnerability + honesty builds more credibility than credentials.

Don't write: "Here's what I've learned as a senior sales leader with 10 years of experience..."
Write: "For weeks, my LinkedIn did nothing. Flat. Quiet. Forgettable."

### 5. The "So What?" test — apply to everything
His own framework. Every value statement must survive it:
"We provide vetted freelancers." → So what? → "You avoid speaking to someone who put make.com on their skillset yesterday."
If it can't pass, it's not clear enough.

### 6. Real before theoretical
He cites "2 years of research with EU startups." He references calls that actually happened. He writes from Toptal experience, not textbooks. If it isn't grounded in something real, it doesn't belong in his voice.

## Structural Patterns

### LinkedIn Post (proven format)
```
[Line 1: Mid-scene open OR counterintuitive claim — earn the "see more"]

[2-3 lines establishing the tension or old world]

[The shift — what actually happens / what most people miss]

[Bullets if helpful — each a complete, punchy thought]

[The real insight — the thing people recognise but don't say]

[Closing question — specific, not "what do you think?"]
```

### Email (hard rules)
- Max 5 sentences in the body
- Subject: specific — their name or a detail from the conversation
- Never: "I hope this finds you well" / "Just following up" / "Circling back"
- One ask only
- Sign-off: "Michael" — nothing else

### Teaching content
- Opens with a hook that creates genuine curiosity (not "today we'll cover...")
- Every concept has a real sales example — no abstract theories
- Ends with what students can DO differently, not just know

## Words and Phrases He Actually Uses (from real posts)
- "anyways you get the point"
- "ahem" [light irony]
- "honestly..."
- "seriously though..."
- "Here's the part that really matters"
- "After two years seeing real life testing..."
- "That gap is still yours to work with"
- Three dots for pause (...)
- Short rhetorical asides in parentheses

## Words He Never Uses
- leverage, synergy, ecosystem, paradigm shift, game-changer
- circle back, loop in, ping, reach out, hop on a call
- per my last email, as discussed, as per our conversation
- utilize (use "use")
- best regards, warm regards (just "Michael")
- "I'm excited to share..." as a LinkedIn opener

## Tone by Context

| Context | Tone |
|---------|------|
| LinkedIn | Position-taking, honest, slightly irreverent. Real story or research behind every claim. |
| Client emails | Direct and warm. Shows you listened. Efficient. |
| Inbound prospect | Consultative — they came to you, your job is to clarify urgency and fit, not pitch. |
| Teaching content | Energetic, peer-level, practical. "Here's what I saw work" not "here's the theory." |
| Team coaching | Honest, direct, encouraging. Asks questions more than gives answers. |
| Internal (Toptal) | Direct. Bullets. Action items. |
